# Idea-Discovery Methodology Playbook
*Distilled from a 10-iteration, ~430-idea, ~65-verification empirical run (2026). Every rule below was paid for with a real kill or confirm.*

## 1. The criteria (the filter everything must pass)
1. **Novel at market level** — no publicly known product **or project** (including government/academic with deployment intent) addressing the same issue or providing the same service.
2. **Tech base** — AI, Blockchain, or Robotics (existing proven components).
3. **Commercialisable** — nameable payer + revenue mechanism.
4. **No R&D** — engineering/integration of proven components only; open research problems disqualify.
5. **Provable demand** — documented spend on a manual/inferior alternative.
6. **Bigdata/HPC** — >1TB data (input, asset, or service) and/or HPC in development or operation — *honestly assessed*.

## 2. The winning pattern (all 10 confirms fit it)
> **An overlooked private-sector stakeholder in an existing money flow** + **an expensive per-instance professional service as the current alternative** (consultant study, survey, test well, manual research: $5k–$150k) + **a TB-scale public or obtainable substrate** (document images, imagery, point clouds, dumps) + **a niche with zero academic/startup attention**.

Corollary: "Replace the consulting engagement with a data product." The consultant's existence is your demand proof, not your competitor.

## 3. Demand-evidence hierarchy (best → worst)
1. Government procurement docs / board minutes / RFPs with dollar amounts (public, verifiable, novelty-safe)
2. Statutory/regulatory mandates forcing recurring purchases (ASTM, USACE monitoring, Part 139)
3. Industry press explicitly naming an unsolved gap ("an opportunity for AI price prediction")
4. Funded startups in the ADJACENT problem (proves category spend)
5. A single-state/single-vertical paid analog (proves the business model)
6. ⚠️ Vendor content marketing = the vendor IS the competitor (self-refuting evidence)

## 4. Generation tactics, ranked by empirical yield
| Rank | Tactic | Yield in run |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rescue/reframe near-misses** — re-scope ideas that failed ONE criterion (different buyer, digitized-subset data, ethical reframe) | 5 of 10 confirms |
| 2 | **Schlep-inventory reversal** (PG) — re-score your own dismissed ideas: tedium-kill ≠ merit-kill (physical archives, county-by-county sourcing) | 2 confirms (RailTitle, CornerVault) |
| 3 | **Gap-statement mining in neutral sources** — government audit reports (GAO/state auditors: pain + $ + tooling absence, neutrally documented); industry press naming unsolved gaps | 1 confirm + best leads |
| 4 | **Substrate-first search** — start from an inherently massive data family (scanned archives, imagery, point clouds, dumps) then find the buyer | 2 confirms |
| 5 | **Recent trends (<12 months)** — new mandates/shifts create demand before incumbents; windows close within months post-2024 | leads only (windows found already taken at 12-18mo) |
| 6 | Review-gap mining (G2/Capterra "none of them do X") | feature-races, ~0 |
| 7 | Indexed crowdwisdom ("I'd pay for…") | ~0 — findable wishes are already-founded startups (CompStak, UpCodes, Albedo) |
| 8 | Trade-press sweeps, forum schlep mining, convention agendas | low — mostly vendor content or covered territory |

Theory base (see METHODOLOGY.md sources): Paul Graham (schlep blindness, organic ideas), Shane (asymmetric/prior knowledge — mine gated practitioner sources), Ulwick ODI/JTBD, Sarasvathy effectuation (means audit — your own corpus is a means), SIT/TRIZ recombination, a16z unbundling, Walling stair-step (validate via solo practitioners — loudest, least watched).

## 5. Kill-patterns (auto-reject at triage; each cost us a verification to learn)
- **Satellite/EO monitoring anything** — saturated (ImiSight, Satelligence, OpenET, Zesty.ai…)
- **Any domain with academic/government data-science attention** (environment, geoscience, ag, civic data) — a "publicly known project" always exists (USGS, CATALOG, AgTile-US)
- **Visible public-archive AI plays** — productized within months (Atomic Canyon, Pentacue, Oseberg)
- **Valuation/appraisal niches** — engine incumbents hide behind the consumer gap (Verisk, Cotality, e2Value)
- **Confidential-input businesses** (surety financials, loss runs, CDRs, smart meters, dam files) — data-obtainability fail
- **Vertical field-service SaaS + "add prediction"** (ServiceCore/Routeware/AMCS class)
- **Revenue management/dynamic pricing for any hospitality-adjacent vertical** (Campspot, PriceLabs, IDeaS)
- **Predictive maintenance, doc-AI staples (invoices/claims/e-discovery), Carfax-for-X, blockchain provenance, sim bureaus, named-regulation compliance dashboards** — mature categories
- **Proven-platform robotics inspection services** — saturated (31-search sweep: every dangerous niche served)
- **Mechanism-siblings where the mechanism is productized for the same asset class anywhere** (vehicle auction pricing existed → impound variant fails; storage was untouched → passes)
- **Free-government-product same-deliverable** (Esri native tools, state mine-map viewers, CSAT, AHAS-when-same-deliverable)
- **Ethics kills**: recidivism/bail scoring, patent-troll enablement, forensic super-resolution as evidence, surge-pricing grief purchases, predictive policing adjacency

## 6. Verification protocol (adversarial, calibrated)
- Verifier agents get 4–6 refutation searches per candidate; must produce named closest-existing + URLs; verdicts per criterion.
- **Calibration** (encode in every verifier prompt — uncalibrated verifiers over-kill):
  - Manual consultants/research services performing the work = **demand evidence**, never novelty killers.
  - Different-deliverable tools (tactical advisory vs compliance artifact; record-keeping vs analytics; hardware vs data product) ≠ same service.
  - Same-deliverable products OR projects with deployment intent (incl. free/gov/academic) = **kill**.
  - Standard ML/NLP/CV on obtainable data = engineering (passes no-R&D); open research problems or legally inaccessible data = fail.
  - >1TB must be honest: document-image archives at millions of pages qualify; GB tabular does not; "TB corpus incidental to a per-unit product" does not.
- Orchestrator overrides verifiers only with documented precedent-consistent reasoning (log every override).
- Parked ideas need **mechanism-level** novelty checks before revival (obituary analytics existed although forecasting didn't).
- Verify the verifier: chase any suspicious URL in evidence lists yourself (the GitDealFlow /wikipedia false alarm).

## 7. Loop mechanics (process that made it work)
- Fable/frontier model = orchestration, triage, final judgment ONLY; cheap models for bulk generation/mining; mid-tier for verification.
- Batch cadence: generate X·10 raw (no evaluation during generation) → orchestrator triage (~75-85% kill) → adversarial verification (~10-25% survive) → extract learnings → update skill → repeat; pivot strategy when a repeated improved strategy yields similar results.
- Log everything (raw batches, triage rationale, verification citations, overrides) in version control — the kill corpus becomes a reusable competitive-intelligence asset and the generator for rescues (the highest-yield tactic).
- Channels exhaust: expect each generation channel to produce for 1–2 iterations then die; the meta-skill is knowing when to pivot.

## 8. The three knowledge pools that stay exploitable
Visible gaps get productized within months. Only three pools reliably resist scraping-driven competition:
1. **Tedium-dismissed ideas** (schleps — physical digitization, county-by-county sourcing)
2. **Gated practitioner knowledge** (member-only content; proxy: convention agendas, CE catalogs — limited tool access reduces yield)
3. **Your own accumulated verification corpus** (asymmetric knowledge nobody else holds)
